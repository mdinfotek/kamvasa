TRADITION & LORE

The vine with small, fierce fruits

Small fruits. Quiet confidence.

Gokshura — the cow’s hoof — is named for the spiny little fruits it scatters across dry ground. A creeping vine of field edges and open land, it hides a long reputation behind an unassuming look.

In the classical cabinet it is a cooling, gently strengthening ally, traditionally paired with desire and vigour — the quiet counterweight to hotter botanicals. The fruits are dried whole; the flavour is faint, the tradition anything but.

We like gokshura for beginners: gentle, forgiving, and easy to weave into a weekly infusion rather than a daily demand.

THE RITUAL

A slow, weekly infusion

01

Steep, don’t boil

Dried fruits in hot water, covered, ten minutes — a tea, not a decoction.

02

Add a cooling ally

Fennel, mint or a slice of amla keeps the cup light and friendly.

03

Think in weeks

Gokshura works at the pace of habit. Three or four evenings a week beats one heroic cup.
