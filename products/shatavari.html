TRADITION & LORE

She of a hundred roots

The queen of herbs, generous by nature.

Shatavari’s name is a compliment: the woman of a hundred roots, a nourisher of a hundred seasons. A wild cousin of the asparagus, it stores its generosity in clusters of tuberous roots dug each dry season.

It is the classical cabinet’s great feminine rasāyana — sweet, bitter and cooling at once — traditionally honoured in rituals of hospitality and in every stage of a woman’s life.

We love it as the balancing counterpart in the collection: where other botanicals push, shatavari fills. It pairs beautifully with rose and cardamom.

THE RITUAL

A gentle daily cup

01

Choose your temperature

Shatavari takes kindly to warm or cool milk — rare among roots, and welcome in summer.

02

Let rose lead

A pinch of dried rose or cardamom turns the cup into dessert.

03

Every day, gently

A small daily cup suits shatavari better than an occasional feast.
